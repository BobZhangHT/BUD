BUD optimisation study: protocol before new simulation results
Date: 2026-11-21 (Asia/Shanghai)

Goal and scope
-------------
Keep equal-capacity uniform-design strata, independent SRS without replacement,
and a stratum-preserving, finite-population corrected nonparametric bootstrap.
No candidate may read expensive outcomes, oracle influence scores, or the
data-generating risk function. Selection of UD anchors uses mixture discrepancy
only. Method development across prespecified simulation settings is distinct
from choosing a design using a cohort's observed outcomes.

Primary estimand
----------------
The complete finite cohort's difference in Kaplan-Meier restricted mean
survival times, integral from 0 to tau=3.0, evaluated at exact event times.
This is a design-conditional validation target. Coverage is not interpreted as
coverage for a superpopulation RMST contrast. Existing fixed-grid bootstrap
theory is retained; continuous-time simulations provide additional numerical
evidence, not a proof of a functional bootstrap theorem.

Development
-----------
Four generator settings: dev_pc1, dev_additive, dev_banana, dev_crossing.
N=640 per group, m=80 per group. 16 independent complete cohorts and 30 repeated
phase-II samples per cohort. Initial screening uses B=0, comparing point RMSE
and the exact design variance of the complete-cohort influence linearisation.
The latter is solely a post-design diagnostic.
Candidates: equal-rank BUD with 4 or 2 samples per cell; inverse-quantile BUD
in unwhitened PC coordinates with 4 or 2; one balanced Lloyd update; PC1 slabs
followed by four-cell conditional UD. PCA retains 85% variance throughout.

Benchmarks and information
--------------------------
SRS; PC1-X with 2 and 4 observations/cell; K-means on every standardized raw X
column, no PCA, target H=m/4 and capped proportional allocation with >=2/cell.
Fine K-means and balanced full-X K-means can be checked as stronger diagnostic
benchmarks. PC1-X is explicitly not called the MVRS paper's algorithm.
The previous project's predicted-influence-process PC1 baseline is retained
in a separate historical-scenario check using the previous data generator.
It receives model-derived information unavailable to primary X-only methods.
It may not be relabelled as a raw-X PC1 comparator.

Selection and validation
------------------------
Select one primary candidate using development diagnostics and complexity.
Prefer the simpler method when geometric-mean standard-error ratios differ
by <=2%; avoid a method with large regret in the PC1-friendly setting.
Lock its exact specification and source hashes before examining validation.
The eight validation DGPs, fixed in scenarios.py, cover PC1-alignment,
multivariate/interactions, reference banana, saddle, disconnected mixture,
irrelevant coordinates, low-variance discarded signal, and a null signal.
No DGP or coefficient is altered after observing validation rankings.
Main validation: N=960 per group, m=120 per group; 24 independent complete
cohorts, 40 phase-II draws per cohort, B=399 replicates per draw.
Seeds disjoint from development. All failed or adverse scenarios retained.

Performance and Monte Carlo uncertainty
--------------------------------------
Report RMSE, bias, basic-bootstrap 95% coverage and mean interval length;
also normal intervals using the same bootstrap SD as a prespecified check.
No CI variant is chosen after looking at validation coverage.
Competitive means point RMSE <=1.05 times the smallest comparator RMSE,
subject to coverage scrutiny. Report the full ratio, not just wins.
Use paired per-unit random priorities for legal SRSWOR sampling across methods.
The Monte Carlo cluster is the complete cohort; use cohort-level uncertainty
for paired MSE differences and for coverage. Do not count all nested samples
as independent evidence about performance across DGP-generated cohorts.

Honesty conditions
------------------
No universal optimality claim follows from UD or equal cell size.
An outcome-aligned scalar score can beat every outcome-free geometric design.
If superiority disappears against balanced K-means or random anchors, explain
that the gain is attributable to balance/locality and do not claim a UD-specific
advantage. Large-N scalability of exact Hungarian matching is not established.
